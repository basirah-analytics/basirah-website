Restaurant Sales Analysis: Finding Where the Money Goes Across Five Branches

Most restaurant owners can tell you their busiest branch and their most-loved dish from memory. What they usually cannot tell you is where the money quietly leaks out. Which branch cancels the most orders. Whether Swiggy and Zomato actually earn more than they cost. Which items look popular but barely move the bill. This is a demonstration project we built to show exactly how we answer those questions with data. It uses a realistic simulated dataset of a five-branch Hyderabadi restaurant chain, one million order records in all, of which 433 thousand completed and paid. The patterns are true to life even though the business is not a real client.

The Problem

A growing chain runs five branches across Hyderabad, sells on dine-in as well as Swiggy and Zomato, and takes both cash and online payments. Sales are healthy, but nobody has a clear picture of the whole business in one place. Cancelled and refunded orders are treated as noise. Delivery apps feel expensive, but no one has checked. The owner is making decisions on gut feel because the numbers live in scattered reports nobody has time to join together.

The Questions We Set Out to Answer

  • How much did the business actually earn, and how much did it lose to cancellations?
  • Which branches carry the business, and which are quietly underperforming?
  • Do the delivery apps help or hurt once cancellations are counted?
  • Which menu categories and dishes truly drive revenue, not just order counts?

What We Analyzed

We worked with one million order records across the five branches, covering the full menu of 32 Hyderabadi items, three sales channels (dine-in, Swiggy, Zomato), and every order status: completed, cancelled, and refunded. The 433 thousand on the dashboard is the completed and paid count, which is the figure the revenue numbers are built on. We treated refunded orders as revenue retained rather than lost, and counted only cancelled orders as lost revenue, so the earnings figure reflects money the business actually kept. The data was cleaned and modelled in SQL, then built into a three-page Power BI dashboard covering the overview, the menu, and operations.

What We Found

The chain earned around ₹32.7 crore over the period. It lost about ₹2.39 crore to cancelled orders, a cancellation rate of 6.80 percent, with an average order value near ₹755.

The branches told a clear story. Banjara Hills led the chain, followed by Gachibowli, the upscale and IT-hub areas where spend runs highest. Charminar behaved differently, leaning heavily on dine-in and local orders rather than delivery.

The delivery-app question had a sharp answer. Dine-in and local orders brought in the most revenue and cancelled the least, around 3 percent. Swiggy and Zomato cancelled roughly three times as often, near 9 to 10 percent, which means a real slice of their apparent sales never turned into money. The apps still add reach, but they cost more in lost orders than the direct channel does.

On the menu, Main Course led the categories, with Starters and Biryani close behind, so no single part of the menu carries the business on its own. The individual dishes tell a sharper story. Mutton Dum Biryani and Chicken Dum Biryani top the earners, with Chicken 65, Nihari, and Haleem close behind them. Most orders were small, one or two items, which points to a clear opportunity in combos and add-ons.

The Result

In one dashboard, the owner can now see earned versus lost revenue, spot the branch and channel dragging on cancellations, and know which dishes to build promotions around. Decisions that used to run on instinct now run on evidence. That is the difference between having data and understanding it.
